NIGERIA NATIONAL DATA REPOSITORY (NDR) A COMPREHENSIVE TECHNICAL,
PROGRAMME, DATA, GOVERNANCE AND OPERATIONAL OVERVIEW

Document type: Plain-text reference document Subject: Nigeria National
Data Repository (NDR) Primary domain: Public health / HIV programme
information Country: Federal Republic of Nigeria Prepared: October 2026

====================================================================== 1.
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================

The Nigeria National Data Repository (NDR) is a national, web-based
public-health data platform designed to collect, integrate, validate,
store, manage, analyse and disseminate patient-level programme
information generated by health facilities and implementing partners in
Nigeria.

The NDR was established to address a fundamental problem in a large
health programme: clinical and programme data are generated by many
electronic medical record (EMR) systems, facilities, implementing
partners and reporting environments, but national programme management
requires a consistent, integrated and trusted view of those data.

The NDR therefore acts as a central repository and data-integration
environment. It supports national and subnational HIV programme
monitoring, surveillance, data quality management, research, programme
planning, reporting and evidence-based decision-making.

The repository originally developed around HIV/AIDS programme data and
has been described as a centralized warehouse containing longitudinal
patient-level information. The published NDR architecture and
implementation documentation also describes an extensible design capable
of supporting additional reportable diseases and programme areas as
national requirements expand.

The current NASCP public-facing description identifies the NDR as
Nigeria’s central platform for securely collecting, integrating and
managing patient-level HIV programme data from health facilities and
implementing partners. The platform supports national reporting,
programme monitoring, data quality assurance, research and
evidence-based decision-making while emphasizing security,
confidentiality, interoperability and governance.

IMPORTANT TERMINOLOGY: “NDR” in this document means the Nigeria National
Data Repository operated in the Nigerian public-health/HIV programme
context. It should not be confused with the National Data Repository
operated in Nigeria’s upstream petroleum sector by the Nigerian Upstream
Regulatory Commission (NUPRC).

======================================================================
2. PURPOSE OF THE NDR
======================================================================

The major purposes of the NDR include:

1.  Centralization of programme data Bringing data from multiple
    facilities, EMRs and implementing partners into a common national
    repository.

2.  National programme monitoring Providing a consolidated view of HIV
    programme performance across facilities, implementing partners,
    states and the national programme.

3.  Patient-level longitudinal analysis Maintaining longitudinal
    information that can support analysis of patient journeys through
    testing, enrolment, treatment, laboratory monitoring and other
    programme events.

4.  Surveillance Supporting case-based surveillance, HIV recent
    infection surveillance and other public-health surveillance
    functions.

5.  Data quality assurance Validating incoming records and identifying
    incomplete, invalid, inconsistent or duplicate data.

6.  Deduplication and patient matching Supporting identification of
    records that represent the same individual, including the use of
    biometric information where available and permitted.

7.  Decision support Converting large volumes of operational data into
    indicators, dashboards, lists, trends and actionable information for
    programme managers.

8.  Research Providing controlled access to appropriate datasets for
    research and secondary analysis.

9.  Reporting Supporting national programme reporting and reconciliation
    with other reporting systems.

10. Interoperability Establishing common information structures and
    exchange standards so that different EMR systems can communicate
    with the national repository.

11. Data-informed resource allocation Helping decision-makers identify
    geographic, demographic, facility and programme gaps.

12. Accountability Providing measurable evidence of programme
    implementation and outcomes.

3. HISTORICAL DEVELOPMENT
======================================================================

Nigeria’s health information environment historically consisted of
multiple facility-level systems, paper-based records, electronic medical
records and implementing-partner repositories.

Before a national repository existed, implementing partners could
maintain their own repositories. Such partner-specific repositories were
useful for programme management but could make national-level
aggregation, comparison, surveillance and analysis difficult.

Stakeholder engagement around the NDR began in the early 2010s.
Published research reports that stakeholder meetings in 2013 considered:

-   data elements;
-   vocabulary and terminology standards;
-   patient-level reporting;
-   technical infrastructure;
-   human-resource requirements;
-   information flows;
-   data exchange structures.

These discussions informed the development of the NDR implementation
guide and its information-exchange model.

The NDR subsequently evolved from an initial repository architecture
into a national data platform used for HIV programme monitoring,
surveillance and analysis.

Published evidence reported that by June 30, 2021, the NDR had
up-to-date records for 1,477,064 patients receiving HIV treatment across
1,985 health facilities. A substantial proportion of records also
contained fingerprint-template information used to support unique
identification and record linkage.

The platform has continued to evolve. Later work described improvements
in ingestion performance, analytics speed, duplicate-patient
identification and data concurrence between facility EMRs and the NDR.

The present NDR is publicly described by NASCP as a secure, role-based
platform with national, state, partner, facility and patient-level
analytical capabilities for authorized users.

5. CORE PROBLEM THE NDR SOLVES
======================================================================

The NDR addresses fragmentation.

A typical healthcare information environment may contain:

-   patient registration systems;
-   HIV testing systems;
-   ART treatment records;
-   laboratory systems;
-   pharmacy/drug dispensing records;
-   paper registers;
-   EMRs;
-   partner databases;
-   state reporting systems;
-   national reporting systems.

Without integration, the same patient can potentially appear:

-   more than once;
-   under different names;
-   under different facility identifiers;
-   with incomplete demographic information;
-   with different dates;
-   with inconsistent clinical information.

At programme level, this can produce:

-   duplicated patient counts;
-   incomplete treatment histories;
-   conflicting indicators;
-   delayed reporting;
-   poor visibility of patient movement;
-   unreliable denominators;
-   weak surveillance;
-   inefficient data-quality processes.

The NDR addresses this by providing a national integration and analytics
layer.

They can contain:

-   patient demographics;
-   enrolment information;
-   clinic encounters;
-   HIV testing;
-   treatment information;
-   drug dispensing;
-   laboratory results;
-   clinical outcomes;

-   other programme-specific fields.

7.3 Implementing-Partner Systems

Implementing partners may operate or support EMRs, repositories,
reporting systems and data-management processes.

4.  Laboratory Systems

Laboratory information can contribute:

-   viral-load results;
-   HIV test results;
-   other laboratory measurements;
-   specimen information;
-   testing dates;
-   result status.

7.5 Other Public-Health Systems

The architecture has been designed with extensibility in mind so that
additional reportable diseases and programme areas can be incorporated
as requirements and approvals expand.

9. PATIENT-CENTRIC DESIGN
======================================================================

A central principle of the NDR is patient-centric data management.

Rather than treating every facility report as an isolated transaction,
the repository aims to construct an integrated longitudinal view of an
individual.

This means a patient’s record can potentially contain a sequence such
as:

1.  HIV test
2.  Positive result
3.  Linkage to care
4.  HIV treatment enrolment
5.  ART initiation
6.  Clinic visits
7.  Laboratory monitoring
8.  Viral-load testing
9.  Treatment changes
10. Treatment interruption
11. Return to treatment
12. Transfer or movement
13. Final programme outcome

This longitudinal model is essential for understanding patient
trajectories and programme outcomes.

The message structure provides a common language between source EMRs and

Published literature describes NDR data as XML encoded and transmitted

14. QUEUE MANAGEMENT
======================================================================

A queue manager can separate incoming data ingestion from downstream
validation and processing.

Conceptually:

SOURCE | v INGESTION | v QUEUE | +–> VALIDATION | +–> ERROR HANDLING |
+–> TRANSFORMATION | +–> LOAD

This architecture helps manage large volumes of incoming records and
prevents one processing component from necessarily blocking the entire
ingestion pipeline.

======================================================================
15. DATA VALIDATION ENGINE
======================================================================

Validation is one of the most important NDR functions.

Validation can check:

-   required fields;
-   data types;
-   valid dates;
-   permissible values;
-   code sets;
-   logical relationships;
-   facility identifiers;
-   patient identifiers;
-   duplicate messages;
-   programme rules;
-   cross-field consistency.

For example:

A viral-load result should have a valid test date.

A treatment record should relate to a valid patient.

A facility should exist in the facility reference list.

A date of birth should not normally occur after a clinic-visit date.

A required identifier should not be blank.

Validation allows invalid data to be rejected, corrected or quarantined
rather than silently contaminating the analytical repository.

Published research reported that the NDR’s validation process
historically operated on a set of required and optional data elements
and that rejection rates improved substantially as the system and data
processes matured.

======================================================================
16. DATA QUALITY MANAGEMENT
======================================================================

NDR data quality management can be considered across several dimensions:

1.  Completeness Are required fields populated?

2.  Accuracy Does the information correctly represent the underlying
    event?

3.  Consistency Do related records agree?

4.  Timeliness Is data submitted within the required reporting period?

5.  Validity Does data conform to the expected format and permissible
    values?

6.  Uniqueness Does one person appear more than once?

7.  Integrity Are relationships between records preserved?

8.  Concordance Does the NDR agree with the originating EMR or reporting
    system?

9.  Coverage Are expected facilities and populations represented?

10. Continuity Can the patient’s longitudinal history be reconstructed?

17. DUPLICATION AND PATIENT MATCHING
======================================================================

Patient duplication is a major challenge in national health information
systems.

A patient may appear more than once because of:

-   different names;
-   spelling differences;
-   changes in demographic information;
-   different facilities;
-   different patient identifiers;
-   missing identifiers;
-   relocation;
-   transfer of care;
-   data-entry errors.

NDR matching can use multiple attributes.

Potential matching attributes include:

-   name;
-   date of birth;
-   sex;
-   facility;
-   patient identifiers;
-   demographic information;
-   biometric information where authorized.

The matching process can identify:

A. exact matches; B. probable matches; C. possible matches; D. records
requiring human review.

======================================================================
18. BIOMETRICS
======================================================================

Biometric information has been used within the NDR environment to
support unique patient identification and record linkage.

Published research describes fingerprint template information being used
to help identify duplicate records and improve uniqueness.

Biometrics can provide a stronger matching signal than names alone, but
they also introduce significant privacy, security and governance
requirements.

A biometric-enabled architecture should therefore address:

-   purpose limitation;
-   lawful processing;
-   consent or other applicable legal basis;
-   secure template storage;
-   encryption;
-   access control;
-   audit trails;
-   retention;
-   breach management;
-   matching governance;
-   false-match risk;
-   false non-match risk;
-   human review.

20. LONGITUDINAL DATA
======================================================================

Longitudinal data is one of the NDR’s most important characteristics.

Instead of asking only:

“How many patients were reported this month?”

the NDR can support questions such as:

-   How many patients started treatment?
-   How many remained on treatment?
-   How many interrupted treatment?
-   How many returned?
-   How many received viral-load testing?
-   How many achieved viral suppression?
-   Which facilities have gaps?
-   How do outcomes vary by age and sex?
-   How have indicators changed over time?

Longitudinal analysis allows programme managers to move from simple
counts to patient journeys and programme trajectories.

22. 95-95-95 CASCADE
======================================================================

The NDR supports monitoring of the HIV treatment cascade commonly
expressed as the 95-95-95 framework.

The three stages are conceptually:

1.  First 95 Percentage of people living with HIV who know their HIV
    status.

2.  Second 95 Percentage of people who know their status and are
    receiving treatment.

3.  Third 95 Percentage of people receiving treatment who are virally
    suppressed.

The NDR can support analysis of the underlying programme data used to
understand performance against these stages.

======================================================================
27. CASE-BASED SURVEILLANCE
======================================================================

The NDR supports case-based surveillance concepts.

Case-based surveillance focuses on individual cases rather than only
aggregate totals.

This allows a programme to ask:

-   Who is the patient?
-   When was the case identified?
-   Where was it identified?
-   What happened afterward?
-   Was the patient linked to care?
-   What treatment events occurred?
-   What laboratory results were recorded?
-   What was the final status?

Case-based data are particularly valuable for public-health response and
longitudinal analysis.

======================================================================
28. REPORTING TRIGGERS
======================================================================

The NDR implementation documentation describes reporting triggers.

A reporting trigger is a defined event that causes a patient’s relevant
information to be reported to the NDR.

The principle is:

EVENT OCCURS | v CHECK WHETHER EVENT IS REPORTABLE | v GENERATE NDR
MESSAGE | v TRANSMIT | v VALIDATE | v UPDATE PATIENT RECORD

Once a reporting trigger is engaged, subsequent relevant disease
information can continue to be reported as updates.

The list of reportable diseases and triggers can evolve as additional
programme areas are brought into the repository.

======================================================================
29. DATA RECONCILIATION
======================================================================

Reconciliation compares information across systems.

Examples include:

-   EMR vs NDR;
-   facility totals vs NDR totals;
-   laboratory system vs clinical system;
-   implementing-partner repository vs national repository;
-   monthly reported indicators vs patient-level data.

Reconciliation can identify:

-   missing records;
-   duplicate records;
-   late submissions;
-   invalid records;
-   mismatched totals;
-   unexpected changes.

======================================================================
30. DATA QUALITY FEEDBACK LOOP
======================================================================

A mature NDR should not simply reject bad data.

The desired process is:

DATA SUBMISSION | v VALIDATION | +—- VALID —-> LOAD | +—- INVALID | v
ERROR REPORT | v SOURCE REVIEW | v CORRECTION | v RESUBMISSION

This creates a continuous improvement cycle.

34. ROLE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL
======================================================================

Role-based access control (RBAC) means a user’s permissions depend on
their assigned role.

Conceptually:

USER | v AUTHENTICATION | v ROLE | +–> PERMISSION | +–> DATA SCOPE | +–>
FUNCTION ACCESS

For example, a user may be allowed to see:

-   only one facility;
-   several facilities;
-   a state;
-   an implementing partner’s facilities;
-   national aggregate information;
-   selected patient-level records.

The exact permissions should be determined by current NDR governance and
access-control policies.

38. NIGERIAN DATA PROTECTION CONTEXT
======================================================================

The NDR should operate within Nigeria’s applicable data-protection and
health-information governance framework.

The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023 established the contemporary
national data-protection framework and created the Nigeria Data
Protection Commission (NDPC).

Because NDR data can include personal and health information, relevant
privacy principles include:

-   lawful processing;
-   transparency;
-   purpose limitation;
-   data minimization;
-   accuracy;
-   storage limitation;
-   integrity and confidentiality;
-   accountability.

Health-sector requirements, government policies, contractual obligations
and programme-specific rules may impose additional controls.

This document is not a legal opinion. Formal NDR implementations should
be reviewed against the current NDPA, NDPC regulations/guidance,
health-sector policies, contractual requirements and applicable
international programme requirements.

39. DE-IDENTIFICATION
======================================================================

De-identification reduces the risk that data released for analytics or
research can directly identify an individual.

Possible techniques include:

-   removing names;
-   replacing direct identifiers with pseudonyms;

70. NDR AND EMR: DIFFERENCE
======================================================================

EMR: Primarily supports clinical care and facility-level operations.

NDR: Primarily supports national integration, programme monitoring,
surveillance, analytics and cross-facility analysis.

A simplified relationship is:

EMR = SOURCE / OPERATIONAL SYSTEM

NDR = NATIONAL INTEGRATION + ANALYTICS + PROGRAMME MONITORING

The NDR therefore should not simply be viewed as another hospital EMR.

======================================================================
71. NDR AND DHIS2: DIFFERENCE
======================================================================

DHIS2 is commonly used for health information management and aggregate
reporting.

NDR is designed to work heavily with patient-level information and
longitudinal records.

Simplified distinction:

DHIS2: Aggregate reporting and health information management.

NDR: Patient-level integration, longitudinal programme data,
surveillance and detailed analytics.

The two systems can coexist and exchange information.

90. MACHINE LEARNING FOR DEDUPLICATION
======================================================================

A machine-learning matching system could calculate the probability that
two records belong to the same person.

Features might include:

-   name similarity;
-   date-of-birth similarity;
-   sex;
-   facility;
-   location;
-   phone/contact signals where lawful;
-   identifier similarity;
-   biometric matching where authorized.

Output:

MATCH SCORE = probability that Record A and Record B refer to the same
person.

Possible decision bands:

HIGH: Automatic or strongly recommended match.

MEDIUM: Human review.

LOW: Treat as separate records.

This must be governed carefully because false matches can incorrectly
merge medical histories.

113. DATA DUPLICATION CHALLENGE
======================================================================

Duplicates can occur at:

-   patient level;
-   encounter level;
-   laboratory level;
-   message level;
-   facility level.

A good deduplication system must distinguish between:

duplicate patient and legitimate repeated clinical events.

For example:

Two clinic visits for the same patient are not necessarily duplicates.

======================================================================
114. FALSE MATCHING RISK
======================================================================

Over-aggressive deduplication can merge two different people.

This can cause:

-   incorrect treatment history;
-   incorrect laboratory results;
-   incorrect patient counts;
-   incorrect clinical interpretation.

Therefore matching thresholds should be conservative and auditable.

======================================================================
115. DATA COMPLETENESS CHALLENGE
======================================================================

A record can exist but still be incomplete.

Examples:

-   missing date of birth;
-   missing sex;
-   missing treatment date;
-   missing viral-load result;
-   missing facility;
-   missing outcome.

Completeness metrics should therefore be calculated separately from
record counts.

======================================================================
116. DATA TIMELINESS CHALLENGE
======================================================================

A facility may have excellent-quality data but submit it late.

Timeliness should therefore be measured independently.

Useful metrics include:

-   average reporting delay;
-   median reporting delay;
-   percentage reporting on time;
-   oldest unprocessed submission.

======================================================================
117. DATA CONSISTENCY CHALLENGE
======================================================================

Examples of inconsistency:

-   patient marked active at one point and deceased later without
    appropriate sequence;
-   impossible treatment dates;
-   laboratory result before patient enrolment;
-   conflicting demographic information;
-   facility assignment inconsistent with the event.

Temporal and relational validation helps detect these problems.

125. EXAMPLE END-TO-END SCENARIO
======================================================================

PATIENT VISITS FACILITY

1.  Patient presents at a health facility.
2.  Facility EMR records the encounter.
3.  Treatment/laboratory information is recorded.
4.  EMR generates an NDR-compatible data message.
5.  Message is transmitted securely.
6.  NDR receives the message.
7.  Message passes schema validation.
8.  Business rules are checked.
9.  Patient matching occurs.
10. Duplicate checks are performed.
11. Valid events are integrated.
12. Analytical structures are updated.
13. Indicators are recalculated or refreshed.
14. Authorized dashboard users see updated information.
15. Data-quality feedback can be sent to the source facility.

126. EXAMPLE OF A NATIONAL QUESTION
======================================================================

QUESTION:

“How many patients are currently receiving ART in each state?”

The system must:

1.  Identify the current reporting period.
2.  Identify the approved treatment-current definition.
3.  Query the appropriate analytical dataset.
4.  Apply programme eligibility rules.
5.  Aggregate by state.
6.  Validate the result.
7.  Display the result.

A simple count of all records would not necessarily be valid.

======================================================================
127. EXAMPLE OF A DATA-QUALITY QUESTION
======================================================================

QUESTION:

“Why did the number of patients from Facility X drop this month?”

Possible analytical workflow:

1.  Check reporting completeness.
2.  Check upload date.
3.  Check rejected messages.
4.  Check facility status.
5.  Check patient transfer events.
6.  Check deduplication changes.
7.  Check source EMR changes.
8.  Compare previous periods.
9.  Produce an explanation.

This demonstrates why a national repository requires both data and
metadata.

130. STRATEGIC VALUE
======================================================================

The strategic value of the NDR is that it can turn fragmented programme
records into national intelligence.

Raw data: “Patient received a test.”

Integrated information: “Patient tested positive and was linked to
treatment.”

Programme intelligence: “Facility A has a lower linkage rate than
comparable facilities.”

Decision support: “Investigate linkage processes in Facility A.”

Strategic action: “Deploy targeted quality-improvement support.”

131. NDR MATURITY MODEL
======================================================================

A useful conceptual maturity model is:

LEVEL 1 - COLLECTION Data are collected.

LEVEL 2 - INTEGRATION Data from different systems are combined.

LEVEL 3 - QUALITY Data are validated and reconciled.

LEVEL 4 - ANALYTICS Indicators and trends are generated.

LEVEL 5 - PREDICTIVE Forecasting and anomaly detection are introduced.

LEVEL 6 - INTELLIGENT AI-assisted analysis and decision support are
introduced.

LEVEL 7 - PROACTIVE The system identifies risks and recommends
interventions under governed human oversight.

137. KEY TAKEAWAYS
======================================================================

1.  The Nigerian NDR is fundamentally a national public-health data
    integration and analytics platform.

2.  It is strongly associated with HIV programme data and supports
    national HIV programme monitoring.

3.  Its architecture integrates data from multiple EMRs, facilities and
    implementing partners.

4.  The platform is designed around standardized information exchange.

5.  Validation is critical because source systems can produce
    inconsistent or incomplete information.

6.  Patient matching and deduplication are essential for reliable
    national counts.

7.  Biometrics have been used to strengthen unique-patient
    identification in the NDR environment.

8.  Longitudinal patient-level data enable more powerful analysis than
    aggregate reporting alone.

9.  The NDR supports surveillance, programme monitoring, research and
    decision-making.

10. Public dashboards expose selected aggregate indicators, while
    internal access is more granular and role controlled.

11. Security, confidentiality, privacy and governance are fundamental
    requirements.

12. Data quality is not a one-time process; it is a continuous feedback
    loop.

13. The NDR is different from an EMR because it operates at a national
    integration and programme-monitoring level.

14. The NDR can coexist with systems such as DHIS2 and other health
    information platforms.

15. Modern technologies such as APIs, FHIR, cloud platforms, machine
    learning, RAG and AI assistants could potentially extend the
    ecosystem, but such capabilities should not be mistaken for
    documented current NDR functionality unless officially confirmed.

======================================================================
138. ONE-PARAGRAPH DEFINITION
======================================================================

The Nigeria National Data Repository (NDR) is a national public-health
information platform that securely receives, standardizes, validates,
integrates, stores and analyses patient-level programme data from health
facilities and implementing partners, particularly within Nigeria’s HIV
programme. It provides a national longitudinal view of programme data
and supports data quality, patient matching and deduplication,
surveillance, treatment and laboratory monitoring, reporting,
dashboards, research and evidence-based decision-making, while applying
role-based access, privacy, security, interoperability and governance
controls.
